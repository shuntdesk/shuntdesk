What really limits you

An order may move the pool's price 8% at most, and it has to survive its own slippage and fees. At 100x a position dies on a 0.8% move, so only a small order on a very deep pool can be opened that high. The form shows the largest size that opens at the leverage you pick.

FAQ

Asked
at the platform.

Is any real money involved?

No. Every balance, position, fee and liquidation on this site is simulated. Nothing is bought, sold or borrowed anywhere, and there is no wallet to connect.

Then what is real?

The data. Prices and liquidity come from live pools on Robinhood Chain, holders and transactions from the chain explorer, contract code from the chain itself, and a sell simulation and tax reading from GoPlus. A paper trade is filled at the live price, moved by the size of the order against the real depth of the pool.

What can I trade on the paper desk?

Four markets. Majors such as BTC and ETH, priced on every trade from Binance's public stream. Stock tokens such as NVDA and TSLA that trade on Robinhood Chain around the clock. The busiest coins on Robinhood Chain right now. And any token on the chain, by pasting its contract address. Stock tokens come from a fixed list of contract addresses, because look-alike contracts exist.

By fixed rules, not by a model. Each of the seven stops can take points off a score that starts at 100. A token under an hour old, with under 50 holders, or with a stop that could not be read, cannot be called clean.

Can a verdict be wrong?

Yes. The checks read what is public and miss what is not. A clean verdict is not a reason to buy and a danger verdict is not proof of fraud. The desk page shows how past verdicts held up an hour and a day later.

Who are the crew?

Four fixed rules with names. The dispatcher picks tickets, the sizer puts 4% of equity on each, the signal fills it, and the guard closes it at a stop, a target, a liquidation or after a day. They trade the tickets visitors run, but not on trust: the server re-reads price, liquidity and pool age and runs its own security check, and a ticket it cannot confirm is filed as risky and left alone.

How are slippage and fees worked out?

An order moves the price by twice its size divided by the pool's liquidity, and an order that would move it more than 8% is refused. A fee of 0.1% of position size is charged going in and again going out. Borrowed size costs 0.02% an hour.

When is a position liquidated?

When 90% of its collateral is gone after fees and borrow cost. At 100x that is a move of under 1%, so only small orders on deep pools survive their own slippage; the form shows the health a position would open with and refuses one that would be liquidated on entry. On your own desk, majors are marked on every trade and chain tokens at the price of their last swap on chain, read every three seconds, while the page is open; if you leave, it is marked at the price it finds when you come back. The crew desk is marked every minute.

What does the conductor do?

It is a language model that explains: a verdict, an order before you open it, or the state of the crew desk. It is given only the figures on the page, it does not advise, and it cannot open or close anything. If it is unavailable, a plain rule-written answer is shown instead.

What happens when my desk runs out?

Every desk starts with 10,000 paper USDG. When its total value, free balance plus everything in open positions, falls under 100, it is reset to 10,000 and the reset is counted. A desk with no free balance but money still in positions is not reset: close a position to free its collateral. The count shows next to your handle on the board of desks.

Is the board of desks verified?

No. Each browser reports its own paper equity under a handle. Treat it as a game.

What do you keep about me?

Your paper desk and recent tickets stay in your browser. The site's server receives the token address and verdict of a ticket you run, a handle if you post one, and the text of a question you ask the conductor. No account, no wallet, no key.
